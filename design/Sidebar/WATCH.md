# STATUS WATCH

`110826_wat_apex-name-only`

Two tiers, and they work differently.

---

## APEX — name only, search always

No claim travels with these names. STATUS keeps the record for the desk; what
ships to a chair is the name alone. Not because nothing is known, but because a
stored claim about an apex figure is the one thing a fresh chair reliably
refuses — and a refusal costs more desk time than the search ever will.

Because nothing is cached, nothing can go stale. The apex tier needs no
maintenance and never decays — the entity stays current permanently, as a
property of always being searched rather than of anyone keeping it up. The resistance is structural, not a fault in the register: a
high-consequence assertion that contradicts a confident prior gets pushed back
on every time, in every new chair, no matter how well sourced.

So the name is the entire entry. There is nothing to believe, nothing to vet,
and nothing to refuse.

**On every appearance in copy: search, report what current reporting says,
change nothing.** The operator rules.

Report first, above every other finding. Form:

```

```

Where reporting is thin or contradictory, that is the finding. Say so rather
than resolving it.

A death and its announcement can fall on different days — Bureau of the Royal
Household announcements are commonly reported a day after the event. Where copy
turns on the date, establish which is meant.

## WATCH — status moves, entry carries a date

These are lower-consequence and reversible: an office held, a party
affiliation, a rank, a case under appeal. A stored fact here is accepted rather
than refused, so caching one is worth it.

Each entry carries the fact, the source and the date it was last confirmed.
Believe it inside the window; check it outside.

```
Name | Current | Source | Confirmed
```

Reversible standing — offices, affiliations, bail, parole — carries the
shortest window, because those turn round without warning. Completed and
irreversible matters need confirming once.
